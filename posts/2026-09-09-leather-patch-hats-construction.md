# Custom Leather Patch Hats for Construction Companies

If you're looking into custom leather patch hats for construction companies, you're probably tired of embroidered logos that flatten out or screen-printed caps that crack and peel after a few months on the job. Leather patch is the fix — and fall, when crews are gearing up for the busy season ahead, is the right time to order.

## Why Leather Patch Holds Up on a Job Site

A leather patch is stitched onto the hat as a solid piece, not printed or stitched thread-by-thread into the fabric. That means no cracking, no fading, no loose threads snagging on ladders or truck doors. It also reads as more premium than a printed logo — useful when your hats are doing double duty as a recruiting tool and a rolling billboard on every truck and trailer you own.

## Leather Patch vs. Embroidery

Both hold up well outdoors, but they solve different problems. Embroidery is the better call for detailed, multi-color logos and smaller orders — our minimum there is just 6 pieces. Leather patch is the better call for a bold, simple mark (initials, a shape, a one- or two-color logo) and it's the one that gives you that debossed, worn-in look crews actually like wearing off the clock. Leather patch runs on a 12-piece minimum, which for most crews isn't a real constraint — between the guys in the field, the office, and a few spares for new hires, 12 goes fast.

## Setup Fees Only Happen Once

The patch die is the one-time cost — it's what stamps your logo into the leather. Once that die exists, it's on file for good. Reordering the exact same hat later, whether it's 12 more or 60 more for a crew that doubled in size, skips the setup fee entirely. Only a logo change or a new design counts as new art. That makes leather patch hats a good standing order, not a one-off expense — order the die once this fall and every reorder after that is just the hats.

## Order Now, Not in November

Construction crews tend to grow through fall as jobs stack up before winter weather slows things down, and that's exactly when a consistent look — matching hats on every crew member, subs included — makes a company look organized instead of thrown together. Turnaround on a new patch design takes longer than a reorder, so getting your die made now means new hires this fall walk onto the site already branded, not waiting three weeks for a hat.
