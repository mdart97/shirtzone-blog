## Why embroidery, not a printed logo

Scrubs, polos, and zip jackets get washed after every single shift, often on hot cycles with harsh detergent. A printed logo cracks and fades fast under that kind of abuse; a stitched one doesn't. Embroidery is the right call for anything a staff member wears daily for years, which is exactly why it's the standard for medical offices, dental practices, vet clinics, and home health teams. We break down embroidery versus other decoration methods in more depth in [our guide to choosing the right one](/p/choosing-your-decoration-method/) — the short version for scrubs is: embroidery, every time.

## The minimum is smaller than you'd think

Embroidery runs on a **6-piece minimum**, which covers most small practices without anyone needing to round up their headcount. A 4-provider clinic with two front-desk staff clears it easily. Mix garment types in the same order too — scrub tops for clinical staff, polos for the front desk, a zip jacket for the office manager — as long as the logo is the same, it's one embroidery setup covering everyone.

## What to order before the rush

- **Embroidered scrub tops** in clinical staff's usual colors, left-chest logo
- **Polos** for front desk and administrative staff — sharper for patient-facing work than a t-shirt
- **Quarter-zips or jackets** for anyone in and out of exam rooms or doing home visits, where an extra layer matters
- A couple of **spares in common sizes** for new hires — turnover happens, and having a shirt ready on day one beats a new employee's first week in street clothes

## Reorders stay cheap

Once your logo is digitized for embroidery, that setup is on file for good. **Exact reorders of the same design never carry a re-setup fee** — order 8 scrub tops now and 4 more polos in November when you bring on seasonal help, and you'll only ever pay for the garments and stitching, not the digitizing twice. That's worth knowing before you're tempted to over-order now just to avoid a second setup charge later.
