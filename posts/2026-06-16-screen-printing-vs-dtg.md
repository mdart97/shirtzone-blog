# Screen Printing vs. DTG: Which Is Right for Your Order?

If you've ever requested a quote for custom shirts and been asked "screen print or DTG?", you're not alone in not knowing the answer. It's the single most common question we get at ShirtZone, and the right choice depends almost entirely on two things: **how many shirts you need** and **what your design looks like**.

## Screen printing: the workhorse for bulk orders

Screen printing pushes ink through a stencil (the "screen") one color at a time. Setting up those screens takes real work, which is why small orders cost more per shirt — but once the press is running, each additional shirt is fast and cheap.

**Choose screen printing when:**

- You need **24+ shirts** with the same design
- Your design uses **1–4 solid colors** (logos, team names, event branding)
- You want the most durable print — screen printing ink bonds hard and survives hundreds of washes
- You're printing on dark garments and want colors that pop

This is why nearly every business uniform, team jersey, and event tee you've ever seen was screen printed. At volume, nothing beats it on price or durability.

## DTG: photographic detail, no minimums

Direct-to-garment (DTG) printing works like an inkjet printer for fabric. There are no screens to set up, so a single shirt costs about the same per unit as twenty.

**Choose DTG when:**

- You need a **small run** — 1 to 20 shirts
- Your design has **photos, gradients, or lots of colors** that would be expensive to separate into screens
- You're testing a design before committing to a bulk order

The tradeoff: DTG prints are slightly softer in color on dark garments, and per-shirt cost doesn't drop much at volume.

## The quick answer

| Your situation | Best method |
|---|---|
| 50 staff polos with a 2-color logo | Screen printing |
| 12 memorial shirts with a photo | DTG |
| 200 fundraiser tees, 3-color design | Screen printing |
| 5 prototype shirts for a new merch line | DTG |
