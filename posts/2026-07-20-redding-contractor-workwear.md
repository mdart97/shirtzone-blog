## Why fall is crew-gear season

Summer heat slows some trades down, but fall is when framing, roofing, landscaping, and remodel crews all ramp back up at once. New hires start, old shirts are faded and torn, and job walks in front of new clients go better when your whole crew is in matching, branded gear. If you wait until the work is already stacked up, embroidery and cap orders end up behind everything else on your plate.

## Embroidery: built for daily wear, not one-off events

For crew shirts, polos, and jackets that get worn and washed every single workday, embroidery is the right call. It's a **6-piece minimum**, so even a small 4- or 5-person outfit qualifies, and the stitched logo holds up to job-site abuse far better than a printed one on thick fabric like fleece or canvas. If your guys are in the truck five days a week, embroidery is what still looks clean in month eight. We cover the tradeoffs between decoration methods in more depth in [our guide to choosing the right one](/p/choosing-your-decoration-method/) if you want the full breakdown.

## Leather patch caps: the look every crew wants right now

Ask any contractor what they want their guys wearing and increasingly the answer is a debossed leather patch cap — clean, rugged, and it actually gets worn off the clock too, which is free advertising you don't have to pay for twice. Minimum is **12 pieces**, and bold, simple logos deboss the cleanest. Pair patch caps with embroidered outerwear for winter and you've got a uniform program that looks like a real company, not a job-site giveaway bin.

- **Embroidered polos or button-downs** for job walks, estimates, and client-facing work

- **Leather-patch caps** for the whole crew, 12+ pieces
